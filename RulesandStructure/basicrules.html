Rules and Structure of Football

Basic Rules

Football might seem as simple as kicking a ball and scoring goals, but there’s a bit more to it! The game follows a few basic rules that make sure everything runs smoothly and fairly. Whether you’re new to playing or just starting to watch, knowing these simple rules will help you understand and enjoy the game a whole lot more. Now get ready to learn the simple rules that make football the beautiful game.

  1. Field of Play: Football is played on a large rectangular field with goals at each end. The field has white lines marking areas such as the halfway line, goal areas, and penalty boxes.
  2. The Ball: The ball is round, usually made of leather or synthetic material, and designed to move quickly and evenly across the field. Only the official match ball is used during play.
  3. Team Size: Each team can have up to 11 players on the field, including the goalkeeper, and must have at least 7 players to start or continue the game. Teams also have substitutes who can replace players during stoppages in play.
  4. Players and Substitutions: Outfield players mainly use their feet, head, or chest to control the ball, while the goalkeeper can use their hands inside their penalty area. Most competitions allow up to five substitutions, and once a player is substituted, they cannot return to the match.
  5. Player’s Equipment: Players must wear a jersey, shorts, socks, shin guards, and boots. The goalkeeper wears a different color to stand out. Jewelry or unsafe accessories are not allowed.
  6. Referee: The referee enforces the rules, starts and stops play, and makes final decisions on fouls, goals, and time. The referee also issues yellow and red cards when needed.
  7. Other Match Officials: Assistant referees (linesmen) help with offsides and when the ball goes out of play. A fourth official manages substitutions, while VAR (Video Assistant Referee) can review major incidents.
  8. Duration of the Match: A match lasts 90 minutes, split into two halves of 45 minutes each, with a 15-minute half-time break. The referee may add extra minutes, called “stoppage time,” to make up for injuries, substitutions, or other delays.
  9. Ball In and Out of Play: The ball is out of play only when it fully crosses the goal line or touchline. At all other times, it remains in play — even if it hits the goalpost, crossbar, corner flag, or referee. If the ball hits the referee and changes possession or results in a goal, the referee restarts play with a dropped ball.
  10. Scoring Goals: A goal counts when the entire ball crosses the goal line between the posts and under the crossbar. However, a goal is disallowed if any rule was broken, such as a foul or offside, before it went in.
  11. Offside: A player is offside if they are nearer to the opponent’s goal than both the ball and the second-last defender when a teammate passes to them. However, there’s no offside if the player receives the ball in their own half or directly from a goal kick, throw-in, or corner kick.
  12. Fouls and Misconduct: A foul occurs when a player unfairly kicks, trips, pushes, holds, or strikes an opponent, or handles the ball deliberately (except the goalkeeper in their box). Dangerous or reckless play, such as high kicks or late tackles, also count as fouls.
  13. Disciplinary Actions:
    • Yellow Card: A caution for unsporting behavior, dissent, repeated fouls, or time-wasting.
    • Red Card: A sending-off for serious foul play, violent conduct, spitting, denying a goal with handball or a foul, or receiving two yellow cards in the same match.
    Players shown a red card must leave the field immediately and cannot be replaced.
  14. Start and Restart of Play: A kick-off starts each half and restarts play after every goal. When the ball goes out of play, it is restarted with a throw-in, goal kick, or corner kick, depending on which team last touched it.
  15. Free Kicks: Awarded after most fouls. A direct free kick allows a player to score directly, while an indirect free kick must touch another player first. Defenders must stay at least 10 yards away until the ball is in play.
  16. Penalty Kicks: Awarded for serious fouls committed inside the penalty area. The ball is placed on the penalty spot, and one player shoots directly at goal with only the goalkeeper to stop it.
  17. Throw-In: When the ball fully crosses the touchline, the opposing team restarts play with a throw-in. The player must throw the ball using both hands from behind the head, keeping both feet on the ground.
  18. Goal Kick: When the attacking team last touches the ball before it crosses the goal line, the defending team restarts with a goal kick. The ball must leave the penalty area before another player can touch it.
  19. Corner Kick: When the defending team touches the ball last before it crosses their goal line, the attacking team gets a corner kick. The ball is placed inside the corner arc and usually crossed into the penalty area to create a scoring chance.
