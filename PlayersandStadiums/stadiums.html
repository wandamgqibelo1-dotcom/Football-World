Famous Football Stadiums

Camp Nou (Barcelona, Spain)

Home of FC Barcelona since 1957, with a capacity of nearly 100,000, making it Europe’s largest stadium. Hosted Champions League finals and countless El Clásico matches.

Santiago Bernabéu (Madrid, Spain)

Real Madrid’s fortress since 1947, currently being modernized. Venue for European Cup finals, World Cup matches, and famous comebacks in Champions League history.

Old Trafford (Manchester, England)

Nicknamed “The Theatre of Dreams.” Home to Manchester United since 1910. Known for hosting dramatic Premier League clashes and European nights.

Anfield (Liverpool, England)

Famed for its atmosphere and the “You’ll Never Walk Alone” anthem. Liverpool’s home since 1892. Witnessed unforgettable comebacks like the 2005 and 2019 UCL runs.

Wembley Stadium (London, England)

The “Home of Football.” Historic and modern versions have hosted World Cup 1966 Final, Euro finals, and countless FA Cup finals.

Maracanã (Rio de Janeiro, Brazil)

Opened in 1950 for the World Cup, once held 200,000 fans. Hosted 2014 World Cup Final and 2016 Olympic final. A cathedral of Brazilian football.

San Siro (Milan, Italy)

Shared by AC Milan and Inter Milan. Known for passionate derbies and Champions League nights. Iconic Italian football landmark since 1926.

Allianz Arena (Munich, Germany)

Bayern Munich’s modern stadium, famous for its illuminated exterior. Hosted the 2012 Champions League final.

Signal Iduna Park (Dortmund, Germany)

Renowned for the “Yellow Wall,” Europe’s largest standing terrace. Borussia Dortmund’s passionate home ground.

Estadio Azteca (Mexico City, Mexico)

The only stadium to host two World Cup finals (1970 & 1986). Legendary venue of Pelé’s and Maradona’s greatest moments.
